07 / FAQ

Proxies and network tools are legal products used by businesses and privacy-conscious people everywhere. What matters is whose device and whose network you're on: a school-issued Chromebook is bound by your district's acceptable-use policy, and breaking it can carry school consequences even when nothing illegal happens. That's why every guide labels its risk, every Tier 4 tech confirms you own the device or have the owner's permission before touching anything, and the rollback guides exist. We teach; you decide.

A partial unblock gets some things working — usually the browser, or specific sites — and typically survives hours to weeks before a policy push closes it. A permanent unblock changes the machine itself so the block doesn't come back, and it's only in Tier 3 and up because it's build-specific and needs the rollback protocol alongside it. Every method in the Vault is labelled with exactly which one it is and how long members report it lasting.

Every node is swept every six hours from Oregon, Frankfurt and Singapore: handshake, real page fetch, latency, and a canary request that confirms the node isn't silently blocking half the web. Two consecutive failures pulls a node; replacements drop in within the hour and the swap counter on the status board is public. Friday drops add fresh capacity whether we lost nodes or not.

Tier 1 works on anything with a browser — that's the point of proxies. Tiers 2–4 depend on your build and policy version, so we run a build check: paste your ChromeOS version and policy fingerprint and the dashboard shows which tracks apply to you, including the ones that don't. If your build isn't covered by anything, we tell you before you pay, not after.

Your dashboard lists every outcome the Vault can produce — a working browser route, a named personal proxy, a partial unblock, a full release, a clean rollback, and so on. On Tier 4 you tick any three of them per month. A tech then performs those three on a live remote session while you watch, verifies them with you on your network, and leaves you the rollback notes.

Everything is month-to-month. Cancel in the dashboard in two clicks; you keep access until the end of the period you paid for; your named proxy URL is held for 30 days in case you come back. No retention calls, no guilt emails, no “are you sure?” maze — we made the cancel button the same orange as the buy button.

On personal (Sub One) instances: connection metadata needed to keep the node alive — timestamps, bytes, latency — and nothing about which sites you visit. Shared Wall nodes aggregate the same stats across all users. Tier 4 sessions are recorded only if you ask for a copy. Billing runs through a standard payment processor; we never see card numbers.

The method gets stamped PATCHED with the date, a post-mortem explains what changed, and — in most cases — a replacement ships in the same update. Members on affected tiers get a dashboard ping, and if a Tier 3+ track dies on your specific build, tech chat treats it as priority until it's fixed or rolled back cleanly.
